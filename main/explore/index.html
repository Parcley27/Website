Microsoft Explore internship

Compiled 2026-11-09 from Microsoft's own pages and public job listings. Anything I could not confirm is marked as such.
Short version
  • Explore is Microsoft's summer internship built for first- and second-year university students. Software engineering focus, 12 weeks in the US (8 in India), done in a small "pod" of other Explore interns.
  • There are two separate applications: one for first-years and one for second-years. You pick the one for your year and do not apply to both. First-year applicants can be in the US, Canada or Mexico, so a UBC first-year qualifies on the country rule.
  • You need an intro CS course (or equivalent) and one semester of calculus (or equivalent) completed. No CS major needed.
  • Microsoft publishes no fixed deadline for Explore. Its regular internships are processed on a rolling basis from September to April, so applying early is the safe approach.
  • A community-run Summer 2027 internship list shows a "First-Year Explore Program Engineering Intern" in Redmond added on Nov 1. I could not confirm that posting is open on Microsoft's own site (details below).
Things to verify before relying on this
  • Whether the Summer 2027 first-year application is actually open right now and where the live apply link is.
  • Work authorization for a Canadian citizen in Redmond or Atlanta. Microsoft's Explore page does not say how it handles visas or Canadian students beyond allowing them to apply.
  • The exact pay for Explore (I only found the pay range for a regular university intern) and whether housing or relocation help is offered.

What Explore is

From Microsoft's Explore page: an 8-week (India only) or 12-week (US only) summer internship for first- and/or second-year college students who want to build real engineering experience early. The current track is called Engineering Foundations. You build foundational software development skills through hands-on projects, product thinking, collaboration, and learning how AI supports modern engineering.

  • Pod structure. You work in a pod alongside other Explore interns. You own part of a shared project and are assigned to a specific product team, but there are also talks and sessions on other Microsoft technologies around campus.
  • Where. Most spots are at Redmond, WA, Atlanta, GA, and various India campuses.
  • How it differs from the regular internship. Microsoft frames Explore as for students just beginning their studies who want to learn about careers in software development through an experiential program. The regular 12-week University internship is open to current Bachelor's, Master's, MBA and PhD students, with no first- or second-year focus.
  • Target fields. Computer Science, Computer Engineering, Information Technology, Data Science, Electrical Engineering, Cybersecurity, or something related.

What this means for you (my reading)

  • You are a first-year at UBC, so the first-year application is the one that fits now (summer 2027).
  • Since the two applications are by year, next fall you would be eligible for the second-year application (summer 2028), assuming the program continues in that form. That gives two shots at Explore.
  • The course requirement says "completed" but does not say whether that means at the time you apply or by the internship start. Your first-term intro CS and calculus courses end in December, so this timing matters. It is worth asking the recruiter.

How and when to apply

What is confirmed

  • Applications go through apply.careers.microsoft.com. Microsoft's Explore page links to a filtered view of postings tagged #explorer.
  • Microsoft's regular 2027 internship postings say it "accepts applications and processes offers for these roles on an ongoing basis throughout the academic calendar (September - April)" and that postings stay open until filled. That is the regular internship, not Explore specifically, but it is the best published guide to the timing.
  • The student interviewing page says there is an initial interview and, if selected, a second final interview (see below).

What I found about the Summer 2027 first-year posting

  • The Vansh/CSCareers Summer 2027 internship list has the entry "Microsoft | First-Year Explore Program Engineering Intern | Redmond, WA" dated Nov 01, with an apply link pointing to Microsoft's careers site.
  • When I opened that link, the page loaded but I could not retrieve the posting's details through Microsoft's job system (it returned "not found"). The filtered #explorer search also returned zero results when I queried it today, and the postings listed on Microsoft's Explore page right now are unrelated senior engineer roles.
  • So: there is evidence a first-year Explore posting exists or existed from about Nov 1, but I cannot confirm it is live, who it is open to, or its deadline.
Practical approach
  • Check Microsoft's Explore page and the #explorer search every few days.
  • Apply as soon as the first-year posting is live. With rolling processing, early applicants are generally seen before spots fill, though Microsoft does not promise this for Explore.
  • Have your resume ready now: courses, projects, anything you have built. Create the Microsoft careers account in advance.
  • If it is not showing, email or message a university recruiter (an Early in Profession contact) and ask when first-year applications open and whether Canadian students need anything extra.

Pay and benefits

  • Benefits (official). U.S.-based PhD, University, and Explore interns have medical and vision insurance, paid sick time, paid federal holidays, and software discounts. Puget Sound-based interns (Redmond) also get a bus pass and a fitness club membership.
  • Pay (not published for Explore). Microsoft's Explore page does not state a pay rate. For reference only, a 2027 University software internship in Redmond lists a base pay of USD $5,690 to $11,210 per month (higher in the San Francisco Bay area and New York City). Do not assume Explore pays the same.
  • Housing and relocation. I did not find anything on whether Explore offers housing or relocation help. Ask the recruiter.
  • Pay reference page: US intern pay information.

The interview process

From Microsoft's student interviewing page, which covers early-in-profession hiring in general rather than Explore specifically:

  • An initial interview and, if selected, a second final interview. The process varies by role. For some, you may be asked to write code or share a project or portfolio.
  • All interviews are virtual. An interview day is a series of 1:1 meetings with Microsoft employees with a break between them.
  • Expect both behavioral and functional or technical questions, tied to your experience and skills.
  • Details are sent when you are invited. Your recruiter can say when to expect a decision.
  • Microsoft has prep videos for technical interviews, hardware, data science and business roles on the student interviewing page.

Prep ideas

These are my suggestions, not Microsoft's requirements.

  • Pick two or three projects you can explain end to end: what you built, a decision you made, and something that broke and how you fixed it. A first-year interview is mostly about how you think and learn.
  • Be comfortable writing basic code live: loops, arrays, strings, hash maps, and talking out loud while you do it.
  • Have a link ready to your GitHub or a project page.
  • Get a plan for finishing your intro CS and calculus courses with solid grades this term.
  • Prepare short stories for behavioral questions: a time you worked in a team, got stuck, or learned something fast. Microsoft stresses a "growth mindset" in how it describes its culture.
